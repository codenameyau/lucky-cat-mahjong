How Scoring Works

In Hong Kong Mahjong a winning hand is usually four sets (called melds) plus one pair (called eyes) of 14 tiles. A set is either a sequence of three consecutive tiles in one suit (e.g. 4-5-6 Circles) or a triplet of three identical tiles or quad of four identical tiles. The pair is always two identical tiles. A Hong Kong set has three numbered suits (Characters, Circles, Bamboos), the Honor tiles (Winds and Dragons), and bonus Flowers & Seasons. There are four of each tile and one of each of the 8 bonus flower and season tiles for a total of 144 tiles. Once you have a winning hand, you count faan (番) awarded for patterns like flushes, all-triplet hands, or dragon triplets. Most tables require a minimum of 3 faan to win. The maximum is the limit hand, usually 13 faan.

Advanced Tips

The habits behind the strongest players.

Learn every hand pattern and situational bonus

A win needs 3 faan, and the small ones add up: a self-draw, a concealed hand, no flowers or your seat flower are 1 faan each. Know every hand pattern and bonus in the tables above, including rare ones like Win on Last Tile, Win by Kong, and Robbing the Kong.

Stay concealed

Every tile you claim from a discard gives up the 1 faan for a concealed hand and shows the table what you're building. Some hands need you to stay concealed to count at all: Concealed Triplets, Seven Pairs, Thirteen Orphans and Nine Gates.

Commit to one suit

Once you hold seven or more tiles of one suit, throw the tiles of the other two. A half flush wins more hands than any other pattern, and a full flush pays out more than all triplets.

Be picky with chows

A chow locks in your suit, reveals your hand strategy, and gives up the one faan for a concealed hand. As a rule of thumb, only chow when you have 8 or more tiles of that suit, including honors.

Keep your pairs and your honors

A pair can still become a pung. Always keep a dragon or round wind tile nobody has shown yet. Only discard honor tiles once one or two copies are out. The exception is when playing Concealed Straights.

Know the safe discards

Safest first: a tile someone has already punged, a tile that another player discarded, a tile in a different suit that someone has taken from a chow, or a tile three away from a suit (the 1-4-7 rule).

Adapt to your opponents

Exposed melds tell a lot about your opponents' hands. Stay out of a suit that other players are only collecting, especially your left. Avoid feeding chows. Adapt from discard patterns.

Know when to go for special hands

The big hands pay the most: Thirteen Orphans earns more points a game than any other pattern, then Concealed Triplets and Full Flush, though Thirteen Orphans and Concealed Triplets win in far fewer hands.

Flip your tiles upside down

Flip your tiles upside down to mark which ones you need to pung or discard. It makes it easier to keep track.

Keep your hand flexible to pivot

Don't lock into one hand too early. Keep your tiles flexible so you can pivot from one hand pattern to another, such as a half flush to a full flush. Stay concealed and avoid unnecessary chows, pungs, and kongs.
