# Only Orphans: training progress

The league rounds (1 to 5) were generated by `npm run ml:orphans` (`scripts/ml/orphans.js`). From O5, training runs in the lab (`npm run ml:lab -- --strategy only-orphans`), and its rounds are below: first under the fixed families (lab rounds 1 to 7), then, from the expected-points targets on, a fresh lab trained only on games with the targets. How the model is trained and what it plays is in [the playbook](only-orphans.md).

## Where it stands

- **Games played:** 1,150,000: 250,000 in 5 league rounds, 350,000 in 7 fixed-family lab rounds (the 7th stopped before its result) and 550,000 in 11 lab rounds with the targets.
- **Champion:** `OT11d` (targets lab round 11, OT7c with win bonus 800), +85.1 ± 9.4 points a hand against the rule-based baseline on 40,000 deals (against NC8).
- **In the game:** `OT11d` (`js/mahjong-only-orphans-weights.js`).

## Lab rounds with the targets

The lab started again from OL2c when the rules began picking a target each deal by expected points ([the playbook](only-orphans.md#how-it-thinks)). It trains only on its own games with the targets (data `ml/data/ootgt-<n>`, models OT). Each candidate takes one seat against Neural Cat (NC3, NC5 from round 6, NC8 from round 8, so scores against the baseline don't compare directly across those changes), Jorge Yau and Lucky Cat lc32 on the same 6,000 deals as the champion. The best two play 40,000 fresh deals, with paired margins, and one is kept if J is ahead beyond its standard error (from round 7, also by at least +2, so the lab stops shipping steps as small as OT6e's). From round 12, retrained networks are screened on 12,000 deals instead of 6,000, the two places on 40,000 deals go to the best network ahead and the best play setting ahead, and a candidate must not lose points a hand (the [critique](../machine-learning/iterations.md#critique-the-screen-favored-luck-and-j-favored-wins)). The rule-based baseline plays without targets. After 3 rounds with no new champion, bold arms join, and after 6 the lab stops.

## Lab rounds, fixed families

Each candidate takes one seat at the game's table (Neural Cat, Jorge Yau, Lucky Cat), on the same 6,000 deals as the champion. The best two then play 20,000 fresh deals. J is points a hand + 5 × hands won (in percentage points) against the champion, and a candidate is kept only if it's ahead beyond its standard error. A kept champion ships to the game. Round 1 played NC1 and LC20. From round 2 the table is NC2 and lc23.

After round 6 the lab changed: margins are paired (read from the difference on each deal), the long evaluation plays 40,000 deals (about ±8 points a hand, against ±15), `claimMargin−25`, which can never change a choice, is replaced by `claimMargin+50`, and a new arm, `finetune-wins`, fine-tunes and then leans toward winning. Why: [iterations.md](../machine-learning/iterations.md#the-plateau-after-ol2c-lab-rounds-3-to-6).

## League rounds

Each model takes one seat against Neural Cat, Jorge Yau and Lucky Cat, on the same deals as the best model, the model in the game and the rule-based baseline (9,000 deals, each played from every seat, new deals every round; rounds 1 to 4 used 3,000). Scores are points a hand, with the 95% margin. "Kept" means it beat the best and became the best. A milestone is a best that also beats the baseline by more than the margin and scores more than the model in the game on the same deals: it ships to the game.

The opponents are whatever the game seats: from round 4, Neural Cat NC1, Lucky Cat's network LC20 and the newer Jorge Yau (rounds 1 to 3 played the models and bots before them), so scores from different tables don't compare directly.

## The rule-based baseline

On lab round 2's 20,000 deals, it scored +22.3 points a hand, winning 3.3% of hands and dealing in on 4.6%. On league round 5's ranking deals, the rule-based Only Orphans scored +8.0 points a hand ± 14, winning 3.4% of hands (270 limit hands, 261 Thirteen Orphans) and dealing in on 4.1%, with an average win of 2,747 points.
